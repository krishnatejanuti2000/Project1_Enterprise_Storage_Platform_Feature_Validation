RAID 6 LAB — PARITY FAILURE VALIDATION SCOPE
=============================================

IMPORTANT ARCHITECTURAL NOTE
----------------------------

RAID 6 uses distributed parity.

There is no permanently dedicated physical:

    "P parity disk"

or:

    "Q parity disk"


P AND Q DISTRIBUTION
--------------------

Across RAID 6 stripes, data and parity are distributed across
the RAID members.

Therefore a physical member cannot simply be labelled as:

    /dev/sdb = P
    /dev/sdc = Q

The role of a member varies across stripes.


PARITY FAILURE VALIDATION
-------------------------

The following behaviors were covered during RAID 6 theory:

P parity:
    XOR-based parity

Q parity:
    Reed–Solomon-based parity
    using Galois Field arithmetic


P-ONLY FAILURE
--------------

The theoretical recovery behavior is:

    P information unavailable
            ↓
    Recalculate P from surviving data


Q-ONLY FAILURE
--------------

The theoretical recovery behavior is:

    Q information unavailable
            ↓
    Recalculate Q using the
    Reed–Solomon calculation


LAB EXECUTION SCOPE
-------------------

A separate physical "P-only disk failure" or "Q-only disk failure"
was NOT simulated.

Reason:

    RAID 6 uses distributed P and Q parity rather than dedicated
    physical P and Q disks.

The practical lab therefore focused on:

    - single physical member failure
    - two physical member failures
    - degraded operation
    - degraded read access
    - degraded write access
    - failed-member removal
    - first replacement rebuild
    - second replacement rebuild
    - final RAID health
    - final data integrity


RESULT
------

Parity-only behavior:
    Theory validated

Physical P-only/Q-only disk failure simulation:
    Not separately executed

Distributed parity architecture:
    Confirmed as the correct operational model
