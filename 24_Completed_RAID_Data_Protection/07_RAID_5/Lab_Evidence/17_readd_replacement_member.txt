============================================================
RAID 5 LAB — RE-ADD REPLACEMENT MEMBER
============================================================

Purpose:
Re-add /dev/sdc to the degraded RAID 5 array after the failed
member was removed and its RAID metadata was inspected.

RAID Array:

    /dev/md0

Replacement Member:

    /dev/sdc


============================================================
COMMAND
============================================================

sudo mdadm --manage /dev/md0 --add /dev/sdc


============================================================
OUTPUT
============================================================

mdadm: re-added /dev/sdc


============================================================
VERIFICATION
============================================================

The command successfully re-added /dev/sdc to the RAID 5
array.

Immediately after re-addition, the RAID status was checked
using:

    cat /proc/mdstat


The subsequent status showed:

    md0 : active raid5 sdc[1] sde[4] sdd[2] sdb[0]
          527081472 blocks super 1.2 level 5, 512k chunk,
          algorithm 2 [4/4] [UUUU]


============================================================
OBSERVATION
============================================================

/dev/sdc was successfully accepted back into the RAID 5
array.

The array returned to a four-member configuration.

The following RAID member positions were present:

    /dev/sdb
    /dev/sdc
    /dev/sdd
    /dev/sde


============================================================
RESULT
============================================================

The replacement member was successfully re-added to the
RAID 5 array.

The subsequent RAID status showed all four members present
and healthy.


============================================================
END OF EVIDENCE
============================================================
